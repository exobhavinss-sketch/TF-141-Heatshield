01

Project Overview & Scientific Motivation

The semi-arid Deccan plateau represents one of South Asia's most climatologically vulnerable interior land expanses. Solapur, situated at 17.6599° N, 75.9064° E, experiences an extreme hot semi-arid climate (Köppen classification BSh). Escalating pre-monsoon summer ambient temperatures consistently breach 44.5°C during April and May, amplified by intense solar insolation and deficient green canopy mitigation.

thermostat

Thermal Inertia Trapping

High thermal mass basalt construction and asphalt fractions retain day-cycle solar energy, causing night-time cooling stalls of 4.2°C above surrounding rural peri-urban buffers.

water_drop

Vapor-Pressure Deficit

Pre-monsoon atmospheric moisture surges combine with persistent thermal plumes, driving wet-bulb temperatures into elevated human physiological stress categories.

02

Study Area & Geographic Characterization

The research framework stratifies 100 computationally modeled virtual monitoring stations across Solapur’s administrative limits. The sampling strategy aligns with Stewart & Oke’s Local Climate Zone (LCZ) taxonomy across 9 distinct urban fabric morphologies:

LCZ 2 • Compact Midrise 42 Nodes

Core Commercial Fabric

Bhavani Peth, Mangalwar Peth, and Guruwar Peth. Characterized by high building aspect ratios (>1.5 H/W), canyon radiative trapping, zero setback curbs, and impermeable paving >88%.

Sky View Factor (SVF): 0.28 – 0.41
LCZ 8 • Large Low-Rise 28 Nodes

Industrial Agglomeration

MIDC Chhatrapati Sambhaji Nagar Road and Akkalkot Road Textile Industrial Corridors. Galvanized iron roofing, vast low-albedo hardscape, and waste heat exhaust signatures.

Impervious Surface: 92.4% mean
LCZ 9/D • Sparsely Built 30 Nodes

Peri-Urban Transition

Kegaon University Campus, Degaon, and Hipparga Lake agricultural edges. Mixed scrublands, permeable clay soils, seasonal biomass, representing rural reference boundary baselines.

Vegetation Cover: 34% – 61%

03

Synthetic Data Generation & Simulation Models

science Academic Simulation Notice • Epistemic Integrity

All 150,000 microclimate observations within the HeatShield database are computationally generated using calibrated Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) urban canopy parameterizations paired with semi-empirical thermal inertia models. They represent synthetic computational simulations for urban planning research, comparative policy intervention simulations, and algorithmic resilience benchmarking—not real-time physical telemetry.

Mathematical Modeling Formulations
Urban Heat Island (UHI) Intensity Formulation

Calculated at 15-minute discrete time-steps across all 100 spatial grid cells.

Eq. (1.4a)
ΔTUHI(t, i) = Turban_canopy(t, i) − Trural_reference(t)

Where Turban_canopy(t, i) represents the simulated 2m air temperature at station node i, parameterized by local anthropogenic heat flux (QF), surface albedo (α), and sky view factor (ψsky); and Trural_reference denotes the baseline peri-urban ambient temperature at Kegaon station.

Surface Energy Budget
Rn + QF = H + λE + G

Solves net radiation (Rn), sensible heat (H), latent heat (λE), and subsurface heat storage (G).

Cooling Tree Mitigation Factor
ΔTcanopy = −(κ × NDVIeff + β × SVFreduction)

Empirical attenuation constant calibrated to neem (Azadirachta indica) and banyan roadside canopies.

The monitoring pipeline maps thermal strain using three standardized bioclimatic and remote-sensing indices. The metrics cross-reference physiological stress markers defined by ISO 7243 and IMD heat action directives.
