# HeatShield EDADV — Phase 2 Exploratory Data Analysis

## Dataset and provenance

Input: `Heat Shield Master Dataset(2).csv`; SHA-256 `b9e73ed10d0dc7c824063e7f1b868a95b11a435758f2ed940d63aa30f45e1b83`; 150,000 rows and 57 columns. Every row is labeled `Synthetic simulation` and `is_synthetic=True`. All results below describe synthetic academic simulation records, not verified real-world Solapur measurements. Project documents refer to WRF-Chem/ERA5-Land/IMD calibration, but the source generator and calibration inputs were unavailable for independent verification.

The analysis validated the source hash against the Phase 1 audit, exact 57-column header/order, row count, required fields, numeric parsing and finite values, UTC timestamp parsing, date-component consistency, station metadata consistency, and synthetic flags. No source data were changed.

## Methods

- Numeric summaries use all finite values: count, missing count, unique count, mean, sample standard deviation, median, quartiles, IQR, min, max.
- Tukey 1.5×IQR fences flag candidate outliers. These are retained and not treated as invalid automatically.
- Row-level Pearson and Spearman coefficients use 150,000 complete pairs for each reported pair. Spearman uses average ranks for ties. These records are repeated by station and date, so row-level coefficients are descriptive, not independent-sample inference.
- A station-mean correlation table and station-level group summaries provide a dependence-sensitive descriptive comparison (100 station units). No inferential hypothesis tests were run.
- Monthly and annual time summaries average within station first; intervals are normal-approximation intervals across station means. They describe station variation in this synthetic dataset, not uncertainty in real Solapur climate.
- Spatial visualization shows the 100 source coordinates as points only. No continuous surface or unsampled-area estimate was created.

## Relationships and correlations

The row-level Pearson correlations computed from all 150,000 paired records were: air temperature vs RH, **r = -0.317**; air temperature vs LST, **r = 0.936**; NDVI vs LST, **r = -0.307**; and NDBI vs LST, **r = 0.369**. These are associations within synthetic records. They do not show causation or establish real environmental mechanisms. The full pairwise Pearson/Spearman table, with pair counts, is `../outputs/correlation_pairs.csv`; matrix CSVs and the figure are also provided. Station-mean correlations are in `../outputs/station_mean_correlations.csv`.

## Time patterns and coverage

Parsed timestamps are UTC: `2021-01-01T08:00:00+00:00` to `2025-12-31T12:47:00+00:00`. There are 1,500 observed dates and 1,826 calendar dates in the inclusive span; **326 dates have no records**. There are exactly 100 records on every observed date. Mean air temperature across station means is highest in source month 5 (40.80 °C) and lowest in month 10 (26.86 °C). The daily coverage CSV and figure leave gaps explicit; no values were imputed.

Timestamp values occupy 10 distinct UTC hour bins (8–20 UTC), not a full 24-hour cycle. Therefore an environmental diurnal curve was not interpreted or produced. Monthly and annual summaries were generated by averaging within station first. The source `season` field is summarized using its labels, but the source’s season definitions were unavailable; these groups are not independently validated climatological seasons.

## Zone and station comparisons

There are 100 unique locations and 9 source zone labels. Zone observation counts are: Commercial: 21,000, Educational: 12,000, Industrial: 18,000, Open Land: 6,000, Park: 15,000, Residential: 42,000, Road: 21,000, Transport: 9,000, Waterbody: 6,000. Across station means, highest/lowest air-temperature zone averages are Industrial (35.19 °C) and Waterbody (32.60 °C); for LST they are Industrial (38.47 °C) and Waterbody (29.48 °C). These describe represented synthetic stations, not causes or population effects. `zone_station_comparison.csv` reports station counts, between-station SD, ranges, and descriptive station-mean intervals. The station aggregate CSV provides one row per location with coordinates and within-station summaries.

## Risk categories and spatial analysis

The source `heat_risk_category` values are summarized as encoded labels: High: 37,548 (25.0%), Low: 21,737 (14.5%), Moderate: 77,145 (51.4%), Very High: 13,570 (9.0%). The largest within-zone Very High share is Industrial (16.4%); all zone shares are in `../outputs/risk_category_by_zone.csv`. The CSV/schema provides the field but no independently verified classification thresholds or validation evidence. Counts therefore describe the dataset encoding, not validated health-risk categories. The master data does not include `heat_index_c`, `substrate`, or `status`; none were invented or borrowed from user submissions.

The coordinate map uses 100 unique point locations and station mean LST. Coordinates are representative simulation locations per project documentation. The analysis makes no claims about unsampled places and does not interpolate a heat surface.

## Findings and limitations

- The audited source was read completely; all values in the analyzed numeric fields parsed as finite numbers and no date-component mismatches were found.
- The dataset’s thermal and humidity distributions, relationships, and encoded risk counts are internally describable, but synthetic provenance limits real-world interpretation.
- Repeated station/date structure limits row-level independence; the report emphasizes station-level summaries for grouped comparisons and does not report significance tests.
- 326 calendar dates are unobserved. The timestamps cover only 10 UTC clock hours, so full diurnal inference is unsupported.
- Season labels and risk-category thresholds are present but their definitions were not verifiable in the source materials inspected.
- `heat_index_c`, `substrate`, and `status` are absent from the master dataset.
- IQR flags are screening candidates; they do not imply invalid records. No records were removed.

## Outputs and reproduction

From the repository root, with Python 3.11+, NumPy, and Matplotlib installed:

```powershell
python edadv/run_phase2_eda.py
```

The script obtains the input path and expected SHA-256 from `edadv/outputs/dataset_audit.json`. To specify an input explicitly, use `--csv "path/to/master.csv"`; it must match the audited SHA-256. To regenerate the script’s own existing Phase 2 outputs, add `--overwrite`. No extra packages were installed. Outputs are under `edadv/outputs/`, figures under `edadv/figures/`, and this report is generated at `edadv/reports/phase2_eda_report.md`.
